Abstract

The claim that co-expression of Saccharomyces cerevisiae PDI1 with codon-optimized human proinsulin in Pichia pastoris increases the soluble yield of correctly folded proinsulin by at least 2-fold after 72 h of methanol induction is an open empirical question. This document pre-registers a design to test it.

The primary endpoint is soluble, correctly folded human proinsulin in clarified culture supernatant, measured by RP-HPLC against a native proinsulin standard, with disulfide pairing confirmed in a subset by non-reducing SDS-PAGE/Western blot and LC-MS/MS peptide mapping. The comparison is between otherwise identical proinsulin-expressing strains with and without PDI1 overexpression.

No data have been collected. Expected results below are stated conditionally, for both outcomes, before any measurement.

1 · Introduction

Where the yield is lost

The classic microbial route to human insulin was established in Escherichia coli by Goeddel et al. (1979): A and B chains expressed separately as β-galactosidase fusions, recovered from inclusion bodies, cleaved, S-sulfonated, then oxidatively folded in vitro. The yield-limiting step is that in vitro refolding — reduced chains misfold, aggregate, or form incorrect disulfide isomers.

Yeast secretion avoids that bottleneck. P. pastoris can secrete proinsulin with native disulfides formed in the ER (Kjeldsen et al., 1999; Gurramkonda et al., 2010). The losses move elsewhere: ER folding, ER-associated degradation, secretory export, extracellular proteolysis. Which of these dominates for proinsulin has not been dissected as rigorously as the E. coli route [inference].

PDI overexpression has increased secretion of some disulfide-containing proteins in S. cerevisiae (Robinson et al., 1994) and in P. pastoris (Inan et al., 2006). But the effect is substrate-specific, not universal — Shusta et al. (1998) found PDI/BiP effects depended on which antibody fragment was expressed. PDI1 co-expression is therefore plausible for proinsulin, and not guaranteed. That gap is what makes the question worth a direct test rather than an assumption.

2 · Hypotheses

Three candidates, one taken forward

H1 · selected
PDI1 co-expression increases soluble, correctly folded proinsulin by at least 2-fold.
Mechanism
Higher ER PDI oxidoreductase/isomerase capacity accelerates native disulfide formation and reduces misfolded species entering ERAD.
Strongest prior evidence
Inan et al. (2006) in P. pastoris; Robinson et al. (1994) in S. cerevisiae.
Most likely failure
Proinsulin may simply not be PDI-limited; excess PDI could retain misfolded protein via chaperone activity.
H2 · rejected
Co-overexpressing PDI1 with Ero1 improves oxidative folding more than PDI1 alone.
Why rejected
Adds a second variable and higher cost, and a negative result would be confounded by Ero1-induced oxidative stress and UPR activation. Better as a follow-up after H1 shows a PDI-dependent effect.
H3 · rejected
Lowering methanol induction temperature to 20–25 °C increases correctly folded yield.
Why rejected
Tests a generic kinetic effect rather than the specific disulfide-folding limitation. A negative result could just reflect lower specific productivity, ruling nothing out about oxidoreductase capacity.

Both rejections turn on the same criterion: not which hypothesis is most likely true, but which one yields an interpretable negative result.

Four arms, one variable

6 independent transformants per arm, each analysed in duplicate — the biological unit is the clone, because clonal variation dominates
Power
At within-arm CV 35–50 %, six clones give > 80 % power for a one-sided two-sample t-test at α = 0.05
Pre-specified falsification criterion
Let R = mean Arm B titer ÷ mean Arm A titer at 72 h.

H1 is falsified if R is not ≥ 2.0 and the upper bound of the
one-sided 95 % CI for R, on log-transformed data, is < 2.0.

A point estimate below 2 whose interval still includes 2 is indeterminate, not falsifying. And the criterion is conditional on confirmed PDI1 expression in Arm B by anti-PDI1 immunoblot — without that, the experiment cannot test H1 at all, and no conclusion may be drawn in either direction.

3b · Computational

A computational arm, run before the bench work

The design above asks whether the ER's capacity to form proinsulin's disulfide bonds limits yield. Before committing bench resources, we asked a cheaper question with the same subject: does a protein language model, trained only on sequence, treat those six cysteines as load-bearing? Two independently-known facts are recovered. Sus scrofa sits closest to human (0.55) — porcine insulin differs by a single residue, which is why it served as the therapeutic before recombinant production. Cavia porcellus sits furthest (2.69), beyond both Danio rerio and Xenopus laevis; hystricomorph insulin is markedly divergent.

What this arm does and does not license

It does not test H1. It establishes that the constraint H1 concerns is detectable from sequence, which makes the bench experiment worth running and gives the assay a prior on which residues to watch. It cannot tell us whether PDI1 is the limiting factor in vivo — only the wet experiment can. ESM-2 has certainly seen insulin during training, so this is a recovery of known constraint, not a discovery.

4 · Expected

Both outcomes, written before the measurement

No data have been collected. These are conditional expectations, not findings.

If H1 is supported

Mean Arm B native titer at least 2× Arm A, with the RP-HPLC native peak correspondingly larger and native disulfide pairing confirmed by peptide mapping.

ELISA may also rise, but the decisive signal is the native peak — a folding-specific gain rather than more total secretion.

If H1 is not supported

PDI1 confirmed by immunoblot, but Arm B titer similar to or below Arm A. Worked example: Arm A 10 mg/L, Arm B 15 mg/L → R = 1.5, upper bound < 2.0.

A decrease would suggest excess PDI1 retaining misfolded proinsulin in the ER, or perturbed ER redox balance.

Controls: Arms C and D must produce no native proinsulin peak; spiked standard recovery from Arm C spent medium must fall within pre-specified limits; reduced/alkylated proinsulin must not co-elute with the native standard.

5 · Discussion

What either result would mean

A single genetic modification against an otherwise identical comparator means a positive result can be attributed to PDI1 co-expression rather than to codon optimization, selection markers, or induction conditions. It would motivate work on PDI1 dosage, Ero1 co-expression, and redox tuning — H2, held back deliberately, becomes the next experiment.

A negative result would not imply PDI is unimportant. It would show proinsulin is not PDI1-limited under these conditions. Because PDI effects are known to be substrate-specific (Shusta et al., 1998), that is a biologically interpretable finding rather than a dead end — which is precisely why H1 was chosen over H3.

Limitations

  • One PDI1 source, one promoter, one expression level — no dose–response is established.
  • One induction temperature and a single 72 h endpoint; improvements occurring earlier or later would be missed.
  • RP-HPLC co-elution is strong but not definitive evidence of correct folding for every molecule; peptide mapping covers only a subset.
  • Six clones per arm is powered for a large effect, but P. pastoris clonal variation can still widen the interval.
  • The codon-optimized cassette is identical across arms, so codon optimization is not itself tested.
