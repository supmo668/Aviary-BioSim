Speaker notes

Open with
"Most agent demos reason about a domain. This one measures it." Then the one-line setup: the agent had a protein language model as a tool and a question it could only answer by running experiments.
The environment
Aviary — Future-House's language-agent gym — is the substrate. Its Environment contract is two abstract methods, reset() returning observations plus a tool list, and step(action) returning obs, reward, done, truncated.
Our BioSimEnv exposes three tools: score_variant and embed_sequence, both running ESM-2 (650M) on Apple MPS, and record — the spend meter our own build loop wrote earlier today, exposed via Tool.from_function. Every model call the agent makes is metered by a component an independent sealed test proved correct.
If asked about reward: it is wired to 0.0 deliberately. Aviary carries a reward channel because it is an RL gym; this is tool-mediated discovery, not training. Inventing a scalar to fill the slot would be a fabricated signal.
The measurement
Scoring is the standard masked-marginal: mask position i, one forward pass, read log P for each amino acid. score = log P(mut) − log P(wt). One pass per position covers all 19 substitutions there, so the full 110-residue scan is 110 passes — 7 seconds on MPS.
Figure 1
2,090 substitutions. Cysteine mean −13.02 against a non-cysteine mean of −5.85 across the mature protein — a gap of 7.2 log units. The six cysteines are C31 (B7), C43 (B19), C95 (A6), C96 (A7), C100 (A11) and C109 (A20); they form the three disulfides that hold insulin together.
The honest framing: we did not engineer this result. The direction was known independently — human INS cysteine mutations cause permanent neonatal diabetes through proinsulin misfolding, and the mouse Akita C96Y allele is the classic case. We pointed the agent at a question with an established answer and checked whether it got there.
Figure 2
Two known outcomes recovered by a model that only ever saw sequence. Pig is closest to human (0.55) — porcine insulin differs by a single residue, which is why it was the therapeutic before recombinant. Guinea pig is furthest (2.69), further than zebrafish or Xenopus — hystricomorph insulin is famously divergent.
If asked how we know the labels are right: two of our original accessions were wrong. One "chicken" entry was a rattlesnake fragment, one "zebrafish" was Xenopus. The pipeline now takes every label from the organism UniProt returns and drops anything under 90 residues. A guessed label on a real sequence is a fabricated figure.
The best moment
The agent ran its own controls. Nobody asked it to. After finding the cysteines, it probed C-peptide positions — Gly70, Pro72, Pro79, Glu83, Gln62 — and got scores at or above zero. That demonstrates the effect is position-specific rather than a whole-protein artifact, and the C-peptide is exactly the right negative control: it is cleaved out of mature insulin and poorly conserved.
It also found the second tier unprompted — Leu30, Leu35, Leu105, Phe48, Phe49, Tyr50 — the buried hydrophobic core that collapses around those disulfides.
Traceability
Every stage is a @weave.op, so the trace records which measurement the agent asked for and what it did with the answer. Rounds 1–4 spent 6,905 / 6,304 / 1,087 / 819 completion tokens — deliberation collapses once the pattern is found, which is visible in the trace and not assertable without it.
If challenged
"Isn't ESM-2 just memorising insulin?" Likely in part — insulin is heavily represented. That is why the claim is narrow: this shows an agent can plan and execute a measurement campaign and reach an independently-known answer, not that it discovered new biology.
"Did you seed the answer?" No. The objective names the protein and asks which residues are least tolerant. Cysteines, disulfides and the expected direction appear nowhere in the prompt. The audit trail of all 66 measurements ships with the run.
