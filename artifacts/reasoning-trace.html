Fork 2 · mechanism

Three ways the yield could be raised.

Each hypothesis was generated with its mechanism, its strongest prior evidence, and — notably — its most likely failure mode, before any was chosen.

H1

PDI1 co-expression, alone.

Mechanism: more ER oxidoreductase capacity accelerates native disulfide formation.
Likely failure: proinsulin may not be PDI-limited.

H2

PDI1 together with Ero1.

Mechanism: Ero1 reoxidises PDI, sustaining the oxidised pool.
Likely failure: ER hyperoxidation and UPR.

H3

Lower induction temperature to 20–25 °C.

Mechanism: slower folding kinetics, less aggregation.
Likely failure: lower specific productivity.

Fork 3 · selection — the interpretable one

It did not pick the most likely. It picked the most informative if wrong.

H1taken

PDI1 alone.

One genetic change against an identical comparator. A negative result stays interpretable: either proinsulin is not PDI-limited, or excess PDI retains misfolded protein.

H2deferred

PDI1 + Ero1.

Two variables and higher cost. A null would be confounded by Ero1-induced oxidative stress. Held as the follow-up conditional on H1 showing an effect.

H3cut

Lower temperature.

Acknowledged as the cheapest to run — and still cut. It tests a generic kinetic effect, so a null could just mean lower productivity, ruling nothing out.

Both rejections turn on one criterion, and it is not likelihood of success. It is how interpretable the failure would be.

The selection rule, recovered from the trace — it was not specified in the prompt

Four arms, because two would not have been enough.

Two findings about the instrumentation itself

Instrumented ≠ readable

The build loop emits a span per iteration and publishes successfully — yet querying its traces returned zero.

weave.publish() writes an object, not a call: different store, invisible to the trace query. Six closes also landed as six versions of one object.

Worse: the spec already prescribed @weave.op. The code drifted from it silently, because the wrong call succeeded and printed a confident URL. Fixed; the query now returns 19.

The budget hid the answer

The first runs returned empty content with finish_reason: length. Nothing was wrong with the prompt — reasoning tokens bill against max_tokens, so a ceiling sized for the answer is consumed by the thinking.

Fixed by escalating the budget on empty content rather than guessing. Grounding needed 6,692 tokens to emit 3,072 characters.

Two labels were wrong

Our ortholog set labelled P01334 "chicken" — it is a rattlesnake fragment — and P12706 "zebrafish", which is Xenopus.

Labels now come from the organism UniProt returns, and anything under 90 residues is dropped. A guessed label on a real sequence is a fabricated figure.

What this does and does not show

Read this before trusting the above

  • The trace shows what each agent said its reasons were, not what caused the output. Stated rationale and actual mechanism are different things; only the first is captured.
  • Three hypotheses were requested, so three appeared. The hypothesis space was bounded by the prompt, and the trace cannot show what a wider request would have surfaced.
  • Tokens are a proxy for deliberation, not a measure of it. They separate reasoning-heavy from transcription-heavy steps; they say nothing about whether the reasoning was good.
  • ESM-2 has seen insulin. The measurement recovers known constraint; it does not discover new biology.
  • No wet experiment has been run. The design is assessable from the literature; its conclusions are not, because there are none.

The honest claim is narrow and still worth something: for these runs the decision structure is recoverable. Which alternatives existed, which were discarded, on what stated grounds, and what each fork cost — without re-running anything.
